01The risk you can't see on a statement

The carry gap: sold out of trust, one quiet day at a time

You financed the car. The dealer sold it. Until the payoff arrives, the sale proceeds sit in the dealer's account — unsecured, invisible, and interest-free for them. A dealer who "carries" every sale a few extra days is permanently operating on your cash. When that float grows faster than their sales, you're the last to know — until now.

05Physical collateral

Where is every car, right now?

A floorplan loan is only as real as the car behind it. Field workers run tap-to-verify lot checks; every unit is either seen on the lot, verifiably sold, or explained — a worker drove it, it's at the body shop, it's in transit between lots. And every explanation has an expiry date: a unit nobody can produce feeds straight into the Audit Integrity factor of the score.

Audit rules

  • Seen — tap-verified on the lot this cycle. Clock resets.
  • Sold — payoff pending; past grace it counts as carried cash.
  • Explained — body shop, transit, staff drive. Allowed, but ages: over 14 days it must be re-produced or escalated.
  • Unexplained — missing at audit. Two consecutive misses = collateral event, score hit, immediate re-check.

08Self-insurance

Risk-priced deposits: the dealer's cash covers the dealer's float

A dealer averaging $250K of monthly sales cash flow who carries payoffs six days is, in effect, holding a permanent ~$50K interest-free loan the bank never approved. FloorSight sizes a refundable security deposit to exactly that float — the dealer's own money absorbs the risk the dealer creates, and it's released back as the score recovers. The bank's uncovered exposure trends to zero; the dealer has a direct financial incentive to pay off faster.

09What this buys the bank

From gut feel to a defensible number

Earlier than the audit

Field audits catch a dealer out of trust weeks after the fact. Payoff-velocity drift shows up in the score within days of the first carried sale.

Priced, not guessed

Tie credit-line size, curtailment terms, and audit frequency to the score. Prime dealers earn headroom; deteriorating dealers earn attention — automatically.
